# Canon AI — Coverage Report Spec (draft v0.1 — for reaction, not yet in PLAN.md)

The Coverage Report is the Build-mode flagship deliverable and the top-of-funnel product:
a coverage-style analysis of the writer's own material in which **every load-bearing claim
is a query result over the assertion graph**, phrased as questions and observations, with
citations. It subsumes and productizes R8 (hole-finder) and R10 (setup/payoff tracker) from
SPEC.md and bundles the deterministic check findings into one document.

Industry framing: this is **automated script coverage / a script-coordinator's pass** — an
existing paid, unglamorous service (human coverage runs $75–150/script, days of turnaround)
— not "AI script notes." Writers pay for coverage today and don't romanticize it.

## Position relative to the one rule

Canon still never writes a word of the story. The report contains **no prose suggestions, no
rewrites, no pitches, no dialogue, no scores, no structure prescriptions**. It asks questions
and shows evidence. Rationale (Fabula, DeepMind 2025): writers rated "analysis of what I
wrote" as the most helpful output (W25), asked for exactly this pass on existing scripts
(P2, P6), and rejected taste verdicts, Likert scores, and Save-the-Cat scaffolds. The
defensible quadrant is *grounded diagnosis*; the crowded, low-trust quadrant is *ungrounded
opinion* — which is also what a free chatbot already provides.

## The grounding law (design rule; extends "the LLM extracts and explains; SQL judges")

> **Candidates are generated by deterministic queries. The LLM contributes selection,
> salience-ranking, and phrasing only. A note whose citations don't verify against the
> database is dropped, not fixed.**

Corollaries:
- No note family ships unless its candidates come from a SQL/networkx query.
- The LLM may *decline* to surface a candidate (salience filter) but may never *invent* one.
- Post-phrasing validator re-checks every cited assertion id, scene id, and quoted value
  against Postgres. Any mismatch → the note is discarded and logged for tuning.

## Report anatomy (one document per corpus run, markdown, exportable)

| § | Section | Dial | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Continuity findings | deterministic | `db/checks.sql`, unchanged |
| 2 | Load-bearing canon | deterministic | graph centrality (networkx projection) |
| 3 | Open questions | grounded-subjective | family F1 |
| 4 | Idle setups | grounded-subjective | family F2 |
| 5 | Dormant knowledge | grounded-subjective | family F3 |
| 6 | Corpus appendix | deterministic | counts: scenes, entities, assertions, confirm-queue stats |

Section 2 is the report's only "positive" section and is fully deterministic: the most
connected entities, the facts most cited across scenes, setups that *do* pay off. It exists
because Test B's deliverable must read as pitch-grade coverage, not a defect list — and
because it advertises the graph ("Canon holds 1,412 facts about your world") without
venturing an opinion.

## Note families — v1

Each family defines: candidate query → judgment the LLM applies → citation contract.

### F1 `open_question` — "questions your world doc doesn't answer"

- **Candidates:** union of (a) scene-level `open_questions` already emitted by extraction
  (docs/extraction.md schema — the hook exists today); (b) structural gaps: entities with
  `goal` and no terminating assertion, `secret_of` never referenced after creation, relations
  asserted once with no corroboration, `cannot`/`trait` facts never exercised.
- **Judgment:** which of these would an exec/reader actually ask? Dedupe, rank, phrase.
- **Citation contract:** each question cites the scene(s) that *raise* it and states what the
  graph holds adjacent to the gap ("closest established fact: …" or "no assertion found").
- **Cap:** ≤ 12 per report (the "12 questions" framing from PLAN.md Test C survives).

### F2 `idle_setup` — aging setups without payoff

- **Candidates:** assertions with predicate ∈ {`promised`, `goal`, `secret_of`, `created`}
  (plus emphasized `possesses` — planted objects) whose subject/object has **zero downstream
  references** after more than N story positions (N per-corpus: default 15 scenes or one
  episode, whichever is larger). Pure SQL over ranges; no judgment needed to *find* them.
- **Judgment:** salience only — a throwaway line's "promise" shouldn't headline the section.
- **Citation contract:** the setup scene + quote, the age ("unresolved since 1.08"), and the
  last scene that touched it.
- **Cap:** ≤ 8. Ranked by age × centrality of the entities involved.

### F3 `dormant_knowledge` — asymmetries left hanging

- **Candidates:** epistemic-layer queries: `knows(A, fact)` where the fact concerns B, the
  `knows` interval is long-running, and no scene after its start shows A acting on it or B
  learning it. Also `believes` (false beliefs) never corrected or exploited.
- **Judgment:** salience + phrasing ("Dani has known about the affair since 1.03; it never
  surfaces. Is she waiting, or did the thread drop?").
- **Citation contract:** the scene where the knowledge starts (with quote) + confirmation of
  absence ("no reference found in 1.04–2.12").
- **Cap:** ≤ 6. This family is the moat on display — no codex-style competitor can compute it.

### F4 `unmotivated_turn` — v1.1, EXPERIMENTAL, off by default

Character does something with no established motivation in the graph. Candidate generation
is only half-deterministic (needs scene text + retrieval of all prior assertions about the
character), and the judgment ("is this motivation *sufficient*?") is the most taste-adjacent
in the system. Ships behind a flag only after F1–F3 dismissal rates prove the format, and
its citation contract is the strictest: the note must name the nearest candidate motivation
found in the graph, or state explicitly that none exists. If its Phase 1 dismissal rate is
high, it dies and F1–F3 remain the product.

## Phrasing contract (applies to all families)

- Interrogative or observational mood only. Banned: imperative verbs aimed at story content
  ("make her", "try", "consider adding", "you should"), rewrite fragments, sample lines,
  comparisons to other works, praise adjectives, and any numeric score.
- Every note ends with its citations in the standard `S1E04/sc12` form.
- One note = one issue = one paragraph. No essays.
- Tone register: script coordinator, not development exec. Precise, neutral, unimpressed.

## Note lifecycle & persistence

Notes are rows, not report text. Sketch (final DDL in db/schema.sql when un-parked):

```sql
coverage_notes(
  id            uuid pk,
  note_key      text unique,   -- hash(family + sorted anchor assertion/entity ids); stable across runs
  family        text,          -- F1..F4
  question      text,          -- the phrased note
  evidence      jsonb,         -- assertion ids, scene ids, quotes (validated)
  salience      int,
  status        text check (status in ('open','dismissed','addressed')),
  first_seen_run uuid, last_seen_run uuid, dismissed_at timestamptz
)
```

- `note_key` gives dismissal permanence: a note dismissed once is **never re-raised**, even
  as the corpus grows — same doctrine as `sealed`, separate mechanism (notes anchor to
  assertion *sets*, not assertion pairs).
- `addressed` is set automatically when a later run finds the gap closed (e.g., the idle
  setup now has a downstream reference). Addressed notes move to a "resolved since last
  report" line — cheap delight, and it demonstrates the graph is watching.
- Dismissal rate per family is a first-class metric (below), so dismissing must be
  one keystroke in the CLI, never guilt-tripped.

## Pipeline (extends the existing diagram; no new infrastructure)

```
assertions (Postgres) ──► candidate queries (SQL + networkx) ──► candidate sets per family
candidate sets ──► LLM salience + phrasing (structured output, temp 0) ──► draft notes
draft notes ──► citation validator (DB re-check) ──► coverage_notes upsert (respecting note_key status)
coverage_notes + check findings ──► report renderer ──► coverage report (markdown)
```

LLM calls: one per family per run (candidates batched in), not one per note. Anthropic
structured outputs, no-training config, same as extraction.

## Quality bars & metrics

- **Caps are product law:** report body ≤ 2 pages excluding appendix. A 40-note report is a
  failed report regardless of note quality (Fabula P1: review burden kills adoption faster
  than missed findings).
- **Dismissal-rate doctrine (extends the false-positive doctrine):** subjective notes are
  *more* corrosive than false continuity flags — the writer can't prove a bad note wrong,
  they just decide the tool has bad taste and leave. Per-family Phase 1 thresholds:
  dismissal > 40% across Test B+C corpora → family is pulled back to deterministic-only or
  killed. No prompt-tuning heroics past two iterations.
- **Eval extension:** add planted coverage items to `fixtures/greyharbor/answer-key.md` —
  a deliberately dangling setup, a dormant secret, an unanswered world question — **plus
  decoys that must NOT be flagged** (a setup that pays off, knowledge that does surface).
  Coverage recall and precision join the eval harness the same way check findings did.
- **The Phase 1 measurement that decides the dial:** for every Test B/C delivery, log which
  report sections the writer responds to (edits made, questions asked back, sections
  forwarded). This data — not intuition — sets how far the subjective dial opens.

## Packaging & funnel (assumption to validate, not commitment)

- **Door:** Coverage Report on a single script — cheap or free, shareable, no corpus needed.
- **Lock:** same graph, growing corpus → continuity scans + dormant-knowledge notes that a
  chat window structurally cannot produce; sealed/dismissed judgments accumulate as
  switching cost.
- Test B ($99–199 concierge bible) ships this report as a section of the bible deliverable
  from the first delivery — it is the productized form of what hand-polishing was going to
  produce anyway.

## Dependencies & phasing

- **Hard dependency: Phase 0 exit.** Every family reads the assertion graph; if extraction
  misses the recall bar, coverage inherits the failure. Nothing here starts before the
  greyharbor gate passes.
- Build order after exit: F2 (pure SQL, cheapest) → F1 (extraction hook already exists) →
  F3 (epistemic queries) → report renderer → F4 behind a flag, only if metrics allow.
- Cross-ref: the **retcon ripple report** ("what breaks if I change X?") is a separate
  deterministic feature over the same graph — parked in PLAN.md, not part of this spec.

## Open questions (react to these)

1. **Name.** "Coverage Report" borrows industry weight but implies logline/synopsis/verdict
   sections we deliberately refuse. Alternatives: "Reader's Report", "Canon Report",
   "The Pass". Does the borrowed weight help or invite the wrong comparison?
2. **Section 2 (load-bearing canon)** — keep, or does any "positive" section drift toward
   taste even when deterministic?
3. **F4** — spec'd as experimental; comfortable shipping v1 without it, or is "unmotivated
   turn" the note writers most want and worth the risk earlier?
4. **Bluntness register** — script-coordinator-neutral (spec'd) vs. warmer. Warmth reads as
   taste; neutrality reads as tooling. Test B feedback will tell us, but pick a starting point.
5. **Free tier** — is the single-script door free (max funnel, real inference cost) or $19-ish
   (self-qualifying users, cleaner Test B comparison)?
