# Canon AI — Reader's Report Spec (draft v0.2 — product shape for reaction)

The **Reader's Report** is the Build-mode flagship deliverable and the top-of-funnel product:
a coverage-style analysis of the writer's own material in which **every load-bearing claim
is a query result over the assertion graph**, phrased as findings and questions, with
citations. It subsumes and productizes R8 (hole-finder) and R10 (setup/payoff tracker) from
SPEC.md and bundles the deterministic check findings into one document.

Industry framing: an **automated reader's pass / script-coordinator's pass** — existing paid,
unglamorous services (human coverage runs $75–150/script with days of turnaround; script
coordination is a staffed room function) — not "AI script notes." Writers pay for these today
and don't romanticize them.

---

## Product opportunity map

Four opportunities sit on the one engine (assertion graph + citations). This doc specs #1 in
full; the others are scoped here so the roadmap reads as one arc, not four products.

| # | Opportunity | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reader's Report** (verifiable slice of "script notes") | Grounded diagnostic coverage on the writer's own material — this spec | P1 flagship, gated on Phase 0 exit |
| 2 | **Retcon ripple report** ("what breaks if I change X?") | Writer proposes a change (move a reveal, cut a character, re-date an event); Canon returns every assertion, scene, and check that now conflicts, with citations. The inverse of the continuity scan — runs at the moment of maximum fear (mid-rewrite). Fully deterministic: intersection queries over the changed entity/range. Zero generation. Source demand: Fabula P6 ("can you change this character — what consequences for the beats?") and P2 (TV rewrite velocity). | P1 fast-follow; the closing beat of every demo |
| 3 | **Audience-knowledge ledger** | Track the *audience* as one more entity in the epistemic layer: what the reader knows at each story position. Unlocks provable versions of soft notes — reference-before-reveal (audience premature-knowledge), open-question age (rigorous suspense/dangling-setup grounding). Same machinery as character `knows`; one pseudo-entity. Feeds new report sections + new deterministic checks. | P2 — un-park after report families F1–F3 prove the format |
| 4 | **Canon as consistency infrastructure** (long horizon) | The world-state referee as an API: interactive narrative, branching film, live/devised performance, and game systems all need "keep it consistent with the writer's established world" (Fabula §8.3.3) and none have an engine for it. Canon verifies *other systems'* output without generating. An exit-adjacent asset: the buyer of a writer userbase also gets an embeddable verification engine. | Parking lot with trigger: first inbound from an interactive-narrative team, or wedge stall |

Ordering logic: #1 acquires users on thin corpora, #2 deepens retention on working writers,
#3 widens the provable-note surface, #4 is where the engine outlives any single wedge.

## Position relative to the one rule

Canon still never writes a word of the story. The report contains **no prose suggestions, no
rewrites, no pitches, no dialogue, no scores, no structure prescriptions**. It reports findings
and asks questions, with evidence. Rationale (Fabula, DeepMind 2025): writers rated "analysis
of what I wrote" the most helpful output (W25), asked for exactly this pass on existing
scripts (P2, P6), and rejected taste verdicts, Likert scores, and Save-the-Cat scaffolds. The
defensible quadrant is *grounded diagnosis*; the crowded, low-trust quadrant is *ungrounded
opinion* — which a free chatbot already provides. The real competitor is ChatGPT-for-free;
what a chat window structurally cannot do is (a) hold 60 episodes of canon, (b) cite receipts
verified against a database, (c) remember a dismissal forever.

## The grounding law (design rule; extends "the LLM extracts and explains; SQL judges")

> **Candidates are generated by deterministic queries. The LLM contributes selection,
> salience-ranking, and phrasing only. A note whose citations don't verify against the
> database is dropped, not fixed.**

Corollaries:
- No note family ships unless its candidates come from a SQL/networkx query.
- The LLM may *decline* to surface a candidate (salience filter) but may never *invent* one.
- Post-phrasing validator re-checks every cited assertion id, scene id, and quoted value
  against Postgres. Any mismatch → the note is discarded and logged for tuning.
- Every new note family, forever, must satisfy this law or it doesn't ship. This sentence is
  the guardrail that keeps the door-opener from eating the trust position.

## Report anatomy (one document per corpus run)

| § | Section | Dial | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Continuity findings | deterministic | `db/checks.sql`, unchanged |
| 2 | Load-bearing canon | deterministic | graph centrality (networkx projection) |
| 3 | Open questions | grounded-subjective | family F1 |
| 4 | Idle setups | grounded-subjective | family F2 |
| 5 | Dormant knowledge | grounded-subjective | family F3 |
| 6 | Unmotivated turns | grounded-subjective | family F4 |
| 7 | Corpus appendix | deterministic | counts: scenes, entities, assertions, confirm-queue stats |

Section 2 is the report's only "positive" section and is fully deterministic: the most
connected entities, the facts most cited across scenes, setups that *do* pay off. It exists
because the Test B deliverable must read as pitch-grade coverage, not a defect list — and it
advertises the graph ("Canon holds 1,412 facts about your world") without venturing an opinion.

## Note families — v1 (all four ship in v1)

Each family defines: candidate query → judgment the LLM applies → citation contract.

### F1 `open_question` — "questions your world doc doesn't answer"

- **Candidates:** union of (a) scene-level `open_questions` already emitted by extraction
  (docs/extraction.md schema — the hook exists today); (b) structural gaps: entities with
  `goal` and no terminating assertion, `secret_of` never referenced after creation, relations
  asserted once with no corroboration, `cannot`/`trait` facts never exercised.
- **Judgment:** which of these would an exec/reader actually ask? Dedupe, rank, phrase.
- **Citation contract:** each question cites the scene(s) that *raise* it and states what the
  graph holds adjacent to the gap ("closest established fact: …" or "no assertion found").
- **Cap:** ≤ 12 (the "12 questions" framing from PLAN.md Test C survives).

### F2 `idle_setup` — aging setups without payoff

- **Candidates:** assertions with predicate ∈ {`promised`, `goal`, `secret_of`, `created`}
  (plus emphasized `possesses` — planted objects) whose subject/object has **zero downstream
  references** after more than N story positions (default: 15 scenes or one episode,
  whichever is larger). Pure SQL over ranges.
- **Judgment:** salience only — a throwaway line's "promise" shouldn't headline the section.
- **Citation contract:** setup scene + quote, age ("unresolved since 1.08"), last touch.
- **Cap:** ≤ 8. Ranked by age × centrality of the entities involved.

### F3 `dormant_knowledge` — asymmetries left hanging

- **Candidates:** epistemic-layer queries: `knows(A, fact)` where the fact concerns B, the
  interval is long-running, and no later scene shows A acting on it or B learning it. Also
  `believes` (false beliefs) never corrected or exploited.
- **Judgment:** salience + phrasing.
- **Citation contract:** the scene where the knowledge starts (with quote) + confirmation of
  absence ("no reference found in 1.04–2.12").
- **Cap:** ≤ 6. The moat on display — no codex-style competitor can compute this.

### F4 `unmotivated_turn` — action without established motivation (v1, strictest contract)

- **Candidates:** half-deterministic: for each scene, characters present who take a
  plot-relevant action (deaths, destructions, `promised`/`goal` starts, relationship flips)
  — retrieve all prior assertions about that character as the motivation pool.
- **Judgment:** the most taste-adjacent in the system: "is a motivation established?" The LLM
  judges sufficiency but may not editorialize about quality.
- **Citation contract (strictest):** the note must name the nearest candidate motivation
  found in the graph — quoted and cited — or state explicitly that none exists. No note may
  say a motivation is *weak*; only *present*, *distant*, or *absent*.
- **Cap:** ≤ 5. **Kill switch:** F4 carries its own dismissal-rate threshold (§ metrics); if
  it blows the budget in Phase 1, F4 is pulled and F1–F3 remain the product. Shipping it in
  v1 is a deliberate bet that the note writers most want is worth the trust risk — the kill
  switch is the hedge.

## Phrasing contract — drafted off coding agents

The register writers trust in 2026 is the one they see in code review tools: a coding agent
reports a finding, cites the line, explains the failure case, and shuts up. Port that shape:

- **Note = finding.** One-line summary first, evidence after, citations last. No throat-
  clearing, no praise sandwich, no "consider…" hedges, no flattery, no apology.
  > **Mara's warehouse decision has no established motivation.** She protects Dan in 2.7;
  > nothing in the prior 40 scenes sets up why. Closest candidate: their exchange in 1.3
  > ("You'd do the same for me"), which Dan later contradicts (1.9). — cites: 2.7, 1.3, 1.9
- Declarative for facts, interrogative for gaps. Banned: imperative verbs aimed at story
  content ("make her", "try", "add a scene"), rewrite fragments, sample lines, scores,
  comparisons to other works, adjectives of quality (good/weak/compelling).
- One note = one issue = one short paragraph, structured as: **summary → evidence →
  citations**, machine-parsable so the UI can render summary collapsed / evidence expanded.
- Severity language stays on the deterministic dial (checks). Coverage notes rank by
  salience, never by "severity" — they aren't errors.

## The note surface (the Lovable bar)

The report is rows in a database; the *surface* is what makes it a product people screenshot.
Design target: the writer's first five minutes should feel like Lovable's — upload, watch
something real happen live, get an artifact you can act on and share. Concretely:

1. **Ingest as theater.** Upload → live progress narration streamed from the actual pipeline:
   "Reading scene 12 of 58… 412 facts… 3 characters resolved… 1 open question." The pipeline
   already produces these events; surfacing them turns a wait into the first wow. End on the
   stat line: **"Canon now holds 1,412 facts about your world."**
2. **Script-first split view.** The writer's text is the primary object (Fabula's writers
   rejected plan-first views). Left: their script, scene-navigable. Right: notes anchored to
   scenes — exactly the PR-review mental model: gutter markers where notes anchor, click a
   note → the cited lines highlight and scroll into view. Their words stay center-stage;
   Canon annotates.
3. **One-keystroke triage.** Every note has four actions, no modals: **Seal** (intentional —
   permanent), **Dismiss** (wrong — permanent), **Show me** (jump through the citations),
   **Ask** (opens the chat pane scoped to this note's entities). Triage of a full report
   should take under five minutes; dismissing must never guilt-trip.
4. **Ask-the-bible pane.** The `ask` CLI wearing a shirt, docked beside the script — the one
   Fabula UI element writers liked (complementary chatbot). Every answer cites; citations
   click through to the script view.
5. **The draft-2 moment.** Re-upload a revision → diff view: "3 notes resolved since last
   draft · 1 new · 2 sealed." `addressed` status computed by the graph, not claimed by the
   writer. This is the retention loop made visible — the tool *noticed* their rewrite.
6. **Shareable artifact.** Export the report as a clean, cited PDF/link (writer-controlled —
   rights hygiene means nothing is shareable by default). The Test B bible attaches it; the
   funnel's viral loop is a writer forwarding their own report to a manager.
7. **Visual language:** light theme, industry-familiar type and layout (Highland/Scrivener
   adjacency, not dark dev-tool chrome — Fabula's writers said "dark is very tech"),
   progressive disclosure: summary line collapsed, evidence on expand.

Build note: the internal triage workbench (branch `ui/triage-workbench`, read-only state
inspector) is the embryo of this surface — same data, writer-grade skin. The full surface is
R9 (minimal web app) scope and stays parked until a wedge wins; the CLI report renders the
same rows in the meantime. Nothing in Phase 0/1 blocks on this section.

## Note lifecycle & persistence

Notes are rows, not report text. Sketch (final DDL in db/schema.sql when un-parked):

```sql
coverage_notes(
  id            uuid pk,
  note_key      text unique,   -- hash(family + sorted anchor assertion/entity ids); stable across runs
  family        text,          -- F1..F4
  summary       text,          -- one-line finding
  body          text,          -- evidence paragraph
  evidence      jsonb,         -- assertion ids, scene ids, quotes (validated)
  salience      int,
  status        text check (status in ('open','sealed','dismissed','addressed')),
  first_seen_run uuid, last_seen_run uuid, resolved_at timestamptz
)
```

- `note_key` gives permanence: a sealed or dismissed note is **never re-raised**, even as the
  corpus grows — same doctrine as assertion `sealed`, separate mechanism (notes anchor to
  assertion *sets*).
- `addressed` is set automatically when a later run finds the gap closed. Addressed notes
  power the draft-2 diff moment.
- Dismissal rate per family is a first-class metric, so dismissing is one keystroke.

## Pipeline (extends the existing diagram; no new infrastructure)

```
assertions (Postgres) ──► candidate queries (SQL + networkx) ──► candidate sets per family
candidate sets ──► LLM salience + phrasing (structured output) ──► draft notes
draft notes ──► citation validator (DB re-check) ──► coverage_notes upsert (respecting note_key status)
coverage_notes + check findings ──► renderer ──► Reader's Report (CLI markdown now; web surface at R9)
```

LLM calls: one per family per run (candidates batched in), not one per note. Anthropic
structured outputs, no-training config, same as extraction.

## Unit economics & the free-tier decision

Per-script inference cost (feature ≈ 110 pp ≈ 55–60 scenes ≈ 40k tokens of text; pilot ≈ half):

| Pipeline stage | Tokens (in / out) | Model | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extraction (per-scene, ~58 calls, cached fixed prompt) | ~180k / ~38k | Sonnet 5 ($3/$15) | ~$0.95 |
| Entity resolution pass | ~25k / ~4k | Sonnet 5 | ~$0.14 |
| Report families F1–F4 + narration (5–6 calls) | ~80k / ~14k | Opus 4.8 ($5/$25) | ~$0.75 |
| **Total per feature script** | ~285k / ~56k | | **≈ $1.85** |

Sensitivity: all-Sonnet ≈ $1.35 (≈ $0.90 at intro pricing through 2026-08); Haiku extraction
+ Opus judgment ≈ $1.10; all-Opus ceiling ≈ $2.90. TV pilot ≈ half. Batch API would halve
extraction cost but forfeits the live-ingest theater (up to 1-hr latency), so it's reserved
for bulk corpus backfills (season uploads), not the first-script experience.

**Decision: the first script is free.** COGS ≈ $1–2 means 1,000 free Reader's Reports cost
roughly a MacBook — and each one manufactures exactly the traction evidence a pre-seed raise
needs (reports run, dismissal rates, draft-2 return rate, share-forwards). Willingness-to-pay
is already being measured honestly by Test B at $99–199; the free tier's job is volume and
proof of pull, not revenue. Guardrails so free stays cheap:

- **Account required** (free — email or Google sign-in; Supabase auth per R9). No anonymous
  runs: rate limits, note permanence, and the draft-2 diff all hang off identity, and every
  free report becomes a countable, retargetable user for the raise.
- 1 world, 1 script, ≤ 130 pages, standard queue; rate-limited per account.
- Full report, no crippling — a nerfed free report kills the screenshot/forward loop that
  makes it worth funding.
- Paid (~$20/mo) unlocks the corpus features: multi-script worlds, continuity scans against
  the back-catalog, unlimited draft diffs, bible export, retcon ripple (#2), per-world rule
  authoring. Full launch scope: PLAN.md Phase 3.
- Revisit if abuse or a >$3 real-world COGS shows up in the first hundred runs.

## The funnel (the cold-start fix, explicitly)

Verify mode's structural problem: it needs a thick corpus, but writers arrive with one pilot.
The Reader's Report is the fix — thin-corpus value on day one, graph accumulation as the
retention mechanism:

- **Door:** free Reader's Report on a single script. Broad appeal (every writer wants notes),
  immediate value, no corpus required, shareable artifact.
- **Lock:** the same graph compounds — season two, episode 40, the adaptation. Continuity
  scans and dormant-knowledge notes appear that a chat window structurally can't produce;
  sealed/dismissed judgments accumulate as switching cost. Their canon lives here.
- **Sell:** to users — corpus tier priced against what they already pay (coverage $75–150/
  script; Canon is faster, cited, and cumulative). To an acquirer — a userbase of serialized-
  fiction writers + temporal canon graphs of their worlds + writers' own sealed rulings +
  a verification engine (opportunity #4). The graphs and rulings are the assets a buyer
  can't rebuild with a weekend of prompting.

Fundraise framing: the free tier converts inference dollars into the two charts a deck needs
— cumulative reports run, and week-over-week draft-2 return rate — while Test B supplies the
willingness-to-pay slide.

## Quality bars & metrics

- **Caps are product law:** report body ≤ 2 pages excluding appendix. A 40-note report is a
  failed report regardless of note quality (Fabula P1: review burden kills adoption faster
  than missed findings).
- **Dismissal-rate doctrine (extends the false-positive doctrine):** subjective notes are
  *more* corrosive than false continuity flags — the writer can't prove a bad note wrong,
  they just decide the tool has bad taste and leave. Per-family Phase 1 thresholds:
  dismissal > 40% across Test B+C corpora → family pulled back to deterministic-only or
  killed (F4 first in line). No prompt-tuning heroics past two iterations.
- **Eval extension:** add planted coverage items to `fixtures/greyharbor/answer-key.md` —
  a dangling setup, a dormant secret, an unmotivated turn, an unanswered world question —
  **plus decoys that must NOT be flagged** (a setup that pays off, knowledge that surfaces,
  a motivated action). Coverage precision/recall join the eval harness like check findings.
- **The Phase 1 measurement that sets the dial:** for every Test B/C delivery, log per
  section: seal rate, dismissal rate, addressed-by-next-draft rate, and forwards. This data
  — not intuition — decides how far the subjective dial opens and whether F4 lives.

## Dependencies & phasing

- **Hard dependency: Phase 0 exit.** Every family reads the assertion graph; if extraction
  misses the recall bar, the report inherits the failure. Nothing here starts before the
  greyharbor gate passes.
- Build order after exit: F2 (pure SQL) → F1 (extraction hook exists) → F3 (epistemic
  queries) → F4 (behind its kill-switch metric) → CLI renderer → free-tier plumbing →
  retcon ripple (#2) → web surface (R9) when a wedge wins → audience ledger (#3) → API (#4)
  on trigger.
